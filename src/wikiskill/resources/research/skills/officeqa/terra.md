# Exhaust Treasury Bulletin Tables Before Declaring Data Missing

Do not treat a failed literal search as proof that the requested observation is absent.

For every apparent missing-data question:

1. Write down the requested entity or instrument, measure, unit, and observation period.
2. Inspect the bulletin contents and relevant section introductions to identify the expected table family and table identifiers.
3. Search entity, measure, date, and unit terms separately. Try abbreviations, historical names, and equivalent labels instead of relying on the question's exact phrase.
4. Search recurring generic headers when appropriate, including `exchange rate`, `report date`, `balances`, `total assets`, and `equivalents`.
5. Inspect the complete matching table, its continuation pages, adjacent tables, and neighboring supplied issues.
6. Distinguish the bulletin's publication date from the observation date; a later issue may report an earlier month, quarter-end, or year-end.
7. Reject tables whose scope, units, reporting basis, or period axis do not match the question.

Before answering that data is insufficient, verify that you checked the expected table family, requested periods, plausible aliases, adjacent sections, continuation pages, and neighboring supplied issues. If a relevant table exists but one cell or header remains ambiguous, report that specific ambiguity instead of claiming that the source contains no relevant data.
