# Discriminate Before Choosing

Solve the mathematical question, then audit the answer choices before committing.

1. Parse the target precisely. Identify its quantifiers, domain, requested implication, and any extremal word such as "sharpest," "strongest," "optimal," "always," or "most general."
2. Recall or derive the relevant result independently of the option labels. Do not treat familiarity with an option as evidence that it is correct.
3. Rewrite every plausible option as a contract: assumptions, quantifiers, domain, conclusion, constants or normalization, parameter range, and equality or exception cases.
4. Compare those contracts clause by clause. Check implication direction, necessary versus sufficient conditions, excluded cases, dimensional restrictions, scaling, and endpoint behavior.
5. Try to falsify the leading option with a boundary case, equality case, degenerate example, or omitted hypothesis. Reject a stronger conclusion when it depends on an assumption the option does not state.
6. Identify the nearest competing option and state the decisive mathematical reason it fails. If you cannot distinguish the two, continue checking instead of guessing from wording.
7. Verify the surviving statement positively against the recalled or derived result; eliminating rivals alone is not enough.
8. Map the verified statement back to its choice label only after the comparison is complete, and check that the label matches the intended option.
